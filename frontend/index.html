The question

How much can this portfolio lose — and how would we know if we're wrong?

Every risk number is a forecast. A VaR figure says “on 99 days out of 100, losses stay below this line.” That's a claim you can test, so this project tests it — day by day, out of sample, with the same statistical test a model validation team would use.

VaR can't see past its own history window, so the portfolio is also pushed through real crises. And because market risk is only half the book, 25 companies are credit-scored straight from their SEC filings — with the model's weaknesses on the record.

01 — Market risk

Market Risk

  1. Estimate

    Each day, a 1-day 99% VaR is computed from the previous 250 trading days only — historical simulation, with a variance-covariance model alongside for comparison.

  2. Compare

    The next day's realised loss is checked against that forecast. A loss beyond the line is a breach. The line moves; the breaches stay on the record.

  3. Test

    The Kupiec proportion-of-failures test asks whether the breach count is consistent with 1%. Christoffersen's test asks whether breaches cluster.

02 — Stress testing

Stress Testing

VaR is calibrated to the last year. Crises aren't. Each scenario replays what every stock in the portfolio actually did between two real dates.

03 — Credit risk

Credit Scoring

Five ratios and an Altman Z'' score, computed from each company's latest 10-K, percentile-ranked and blended into a 0–100 score. Fixed bands map the score to five tiers.

    04 — Limitations

    Here's where this breaks.

    The credit model is not a rating.

    It ranks 25 companies on transparent ratios. No business-risk view, no debt structure, no forward projections, no committee. Its agreement with market-implied risk points the right way but isn't statistically significant.

    Thirty stocks are not the market.

    A stated list of US large caps, equal-weighted, long-only equity. No bonds, FX or derivatives — so a “rate shock” reaches this book only through equity sensitivities.

    The backtest has limited power.

    About four years of out-of-sample days means roughly ten expected 99% breaches. Kupiec can't tell a decent model from a mediocre one with counts that small.

    Survivors only.

    The universe was chosen in 2026 from companies that still exist. Replaying 2008 with names that are known to have survived understates what that crisis did to a real book.

    Betas are regime-bound.

    Rate sensitivities come from 2021–2026, when yields and energy stocks rose together. Applied to 1994, they describe this decade's correlations, not a law of nature.

    √time is a convenience.

    Scaling 1-day VaR to multi-week crises assumes independent returns — the exact thing a crisis breaks. Those comparisons are reference points, not capital numbers.

    Check the work.

    Every number on this page is read from a JSON file that the Python pipeline writes — none of it is restated by hand.
